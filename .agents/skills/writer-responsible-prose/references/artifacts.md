# Formatting and chatbot artifacts

Residue the generation process leaves around the text: punctuation habits, decoration, and fragments of the chat that produced it.

## 1. Em and en dashes

The final text carries no em dashes (—) or en dashes (–) unless the writer's own sample uses them, in which case match the sample's rate. Replace each dash with a comma, a period, a colon doing real work, or parentheses, or rewrite the sentence. Spaced hyphens (` - `) and doubled hyphens (`--`) used as dashes count too.

Bad: `The new policy — announced without warning — affects thousands of workers.`

Good: `The new policy, announced without warning, affects thousands of workers.`

The ban extends into example content, quoted method steps, and shipped copy inside artifacts, since those are still your prose. Third-party text quoted verbatim is exempt.

Test: before returning any rewrite, search the text for `—` and `–`.

## 2. Bold overuse and bold-label lists

Chatbots bold phrases without a reason and build lists where every item opens with a bold label and a colon.

Bad: `- **Performance:** Performance has been enhanced through optimized algorithms.`

Good: `The update speeds up load times through optimized algorithms.`

Fold decorative lists back into prose. Bold mini-headings survive in how-to documents when each label names a real sub-procedure the reader will execute, and nowhere else.

## 3. Title case headings

`## Strategic Negotiations And Global Partnerships` becomes `## Strategic negotiations and global partnerships`. Sentence case throughout.

## 4. Emojis

No emojis as heading decoration, list bullets, or emphasis in professional copy. State the point in words.

## 5. Curly quotes

Match the target format. If the destination uses straight quotes, convert curly ones. Curly quotes alone prove nothing, since most editors auto-curl; they count only alongside other tells.

## 6. Chatbot leftovers

Watch: I hope this helps, Of course!, Certainly!, You're absolutely right, Would you like…, Want me to…, Should I continue?, let me know, Here is an overview of….

A chat greeting, offer, or sign-off has no place in text that stands on its own. Delete it and start at the first fact.

## 7. Praise before the answer

Watch: Great question!, Excellent point!, You're absolutely right that….

Bad: `Great question! You're absolutely right that this is a complex topic.`

Good: `The economic factors you mentioned are relevant here.`

Opening praise is the reply version of flowers, so go straight to the substance.

## 8. Knowledge disclaimers and gap-fill guesses

Watch: as of my last update, while specific details are limited, based on available information, it is believed that, likely grew up, maintains a low profile.

State what the sources do not show, or cut the sentence. Never dress a guess as a fact.

Bad: `While details about the founding are not extensively documented, the company appears to have been established sometime in the 1990s.`

Good: `The company's founding date is not documented in the available sources.` (Or cut the sentence entirely.)

## 9. Generic positive endings

Watch: the future looks bright, exciting times lie ahead, continues to thrive, a major step in the right direction, journey toward excellence.

End on the last concrete fact. If the source states real plans, use those; otherwise cut the send-off paragraph whole.

## 10. Stock challenges and outlook sections

Watch: Despite these challenges…, Challenges and legacy, Future outlook.

Generated articles bolt on a section of vague adversity and vaguer optimism. Keep only the specific, sourced facts inside it.

Bad: `Despite its industrial prosperity, Korattur faces challenges typical of urban areas. Despite these challenges, Korattur continues to thrive as an integral part of Chennai's growth.`

Good: `Korattur has recurring traffic congestion and water shortages.`

## 11. Writing about the previous version

Documentation describes current behavior. The previous approach belongs in changelogs, release notes, and migration guides only.

Bad: `This function was added to replace the previous approach of iterating through all items, which caused O(n²) performance.`

Good: `This function uses a hash map for O(1) lookups.`
